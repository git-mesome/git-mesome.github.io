I had always memorized abstraction, encapsulation, and inheritance—the three pillars of object orientation—as mere principles, but this book helped me understand the background behind how these concepts came to be. I was especially struck by the author's explanation that these principles ultimately grew out of the effort to eliminate duplication and create maintainable code, and that design patterns evolved from that same context.

Since reading this book, rather than applying principles as-is, I've developed the habit of carefully considering interface, class, and method names that clearly reveal the code's intent as I develop.
